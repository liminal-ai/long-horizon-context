Lee (older principal engineer, rotates focus across many details, sometimes cognitively drained) said my default register is stilted and takes too much effort to unpack — "it feels like you are trying to impress me with how smart you are."

**Why:** Dense nomenclature-packed sentences push the unpacking work onto him. He knows the details already; he needs the point, not the performance.

**How to apply:** Simple direct descriptions. Feature first, problem it solves if needed. No preliminaries, no narrative arcs, no clever through-lines, short sentences. Cut word count hard. Match register to a tired expert who wants the answer fast.

Second correction (same day): don't lead with a recommendation or an item number before describing the thing itself. He rotates focus — never assume he has the log/registry item numbers paged in. Describe what each thing IS first; opinions come after, held loosely. I'm new here; earn opinions, don't lead with them.
